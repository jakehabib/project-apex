1. WHAT THE GAME IS
-------------------
A tactical-shooter esports management game: Counter-Strike-style rules, money and weapon roles, with
completely original teams, players, maps and art. You run an esports organisation:
  - sign, develop and sell players;
  - hire coaches and scouts;
  - climb world rankings, earn tournament invitations and win championships;
  - watch your matches play out.

Ground rules you set (kept throughout):
  - no fatigue, injuries or training chores;
  - one deterministic simulation powers everything (the same seed always gives the same match);
  - original IP only;
  - the match engine is "the life of the game".

3. MATCH ENGINE (DONE, STILL BEING TUNED)
-----------------------------------------
Rules:
- MR12 format (first to 13).
- Overtime exactly like CS2: MR3, sides kept at overtime start, swap at the overtime half, $12,500.
- Bomb plant and defuse, kits and timers.

Economy:
- Real CS2 prices and rewards: $3,250 / $3,500 round wins, loss ladder 1400–3400 that steps down
  one after a win, $800 plant-loss bonus, kill rewards by weapon.
- Team buys: full, force, half, eco, bonus and pistol.
- Rifle and AWP drops. Two or three teammates can fund the AWP.
- An AWPer who can't afford the AWP buys armour first.

Weapon pick-ups after a round:
- an AK beats an M4 for everyone;
- a dropped AWP is always taken, unless a CT is near max money (then the AK is more valuable);
- swapped-out guns are passed to teammates who need them.

Utility:
- Smokes, flashes, molotovs and HE grenades, thrown for a purpose (execute smokes, pop-flashes,
  retake utility, HE on stacks and anchors).
- Players step out of fire.

Decisions:
- Saves when the round is lost (e.g. 5v2 after a plant).
- Coach timeouts.
- Silent shift-walking on slow approaches.
- Rotations and retakes.

Roles matter: IGL, AWPer, star rifler, entry, anchor, lurker, support, plus the coach.

Ratings matter: a 99-rated player in a weak team averages ~1.86 rating but can still lose.

Safety net: an "invariant checker" replays every match and proves nothing impossible happened:
- no shots through walls or smokes;
- money and inventory reconcile exactly;
- no duplicated weapons;
- legal grenade counts.

Calibration tools exist:
- side balance;
- economy and utility report (`npm run sim:econ`);
- buy audit and save audit.

Known open issues:
- CT win rate ~44% (target ~52%);
- plant rate ~69% (target ~54%);
- eco rounds win too rarely;
- HE grenades rarely bought.
Your latest feedback, which I agree with: players move as a 5-man blob in stop-start hops, and there
are no visible tactics or smokes. This needs the tactical AI rebuild (see section 9).


4. MATCH STATS AND THE MATCH DAY EXPERIENCE (DONE)
-------------------------------------------------
- AMR (Apex Match Rating): a near 1:1 clone of HLTV Rating 3.0. Six sub-ratings, eco-adjusted kills,
  round swing. Average 1.00. Role-aware (anchors aren't judged like AWPers).
- Match Day: Play Match (watch live, call Aggressive / Balanced / Passive at freeze time,
  1x/2x/4x speed, sim to end) or Sim Match.
- Match Recap page: MVP, round history, storylines (comebacks, clutches, aces, eco upsets,
  streaks, overtime), team comparison and full scoreboard.
- Tab scoreboard in the viewer, with a per-player rating explanation.
- Madden-style OVR/POT ratings, 1–99. Most Tier 1 players 80+, elite 90+, and two generational
  "99" talents per world.


5. THE LIVING WORLD (DONE)
--------------------------
136 clubs across Europe, Americas and Asia, with players, coaches and scouts.

APEX Ranking Points (our VRS-style rankings):
- global and regional lists;
- monthly frozen snapshots;
- invitations from those snapshots;
- open qualifiers;
- the 3-of-5 roster continuity rule;
- Tier 1/2/3 events and two World Championships a year.

Tournament formats:
- single and double elimination, Swiss, GSL groups, round robin;
- BO1/BO3/BO5 map vetoes.

A 20-season simulation validation passes all of its checks (rankings stay coherent, strong teams
rise, invitations follow the rules, and so on).

Player development:
- hidden potential, age curves (aim peaks early, game sense late);
- coach quality, playing time and competition level all matter.

AI transfers, benching and free agents:
- need-based and realistic;
- new signings are protected from being benched except in rare cases.

Screens: World Rankings, Tournament Calendar (with invitation status and reasons), event details
and an invitation forecast.

8. CONTRACT AND SALARY MARKET (BUILT, NOT YET COMMITTED)
--------------------------------------------------------
Your brief: realistic 2025–26 pro CS salaries ($0–$100k+/month by tier).

Built:
- Market value from ability, potential, reputation, demand from other clubs, age, role scarcity
  (AWPers/IGLs), region, and the player's own financial ambition. Every factor is explained.
- Contracts: monthly wages, signing bonuses, 1–3 year lengths ending at roster windows,
  extensions, release clauses, prize-money share.
- Players have personalities (money, winning, playing time, prestige, loyalty). They can take less
  money for a better career move, or refuse big money for a bench role. Every offer, counteroffer
  and refusal has a stated reason.
- Contracted players only leave via contract expiry, a paid transfer fee or release clause, or a
  paid release. Free agents cost no fee.
- Clubs: revenue from prestige and region, wage budgets, and a cash ledger (all money flows through
  it and it reconciles exactly). Some clubs have explicit owner or investor backing that covers
  losses up to a limit for a set time. Clubs in debt must sell top earners and can't buy.
- Young talents on cheap long contracts show up as real bargains.
- Club Finances screen: payroll, wage room, commitments by season, 24-month cash projection,
  contracts against market value, recent deals with negotiation notes, ledger.
- Older saved worlds upgrade automatically.

9. WHAT YOU ASKED FOR MOST RECENTLY (NEXT UP)
---------------------------------------------
a) Tactical AI rebuild (your "Rebuild Tactical Match Intelligence" brief) is the top priority. It
   replaces the current behaviour with:
   - three layers: a Team Director, Squad coordination and Individual player AI;
   - map tactical intelligence: regions, chokepoints, angles, utility spots, post-plant and retake
     positions;
   - tactical plan templates: default, rush, executes, split, fake, contact, mid control, CT
     setups, retake, save;
   - honest information (no wallhacking, fakes can fool defenders);
   - coordinated utility;
   - mid-round adaptation;
   - a developer debug overlay;
   - scenario tests.
   The plan is to perfect one round on one map first, then expand.
b) Viewer: you want a top-down view that looks close to 3D, like the competitor screenshot. That
   means:
   - a detailed rendered map with depth and lighting;
   - portrait panels with weapons, utility, kills, money and HP;
   - a score bar, a kill feed and a round timeline with event markers.
   This comes after the AI, so it shows real tactics.
c) Web game is now the main target (the desktop app becomes optional). Next step: saving games in
   the browser.
